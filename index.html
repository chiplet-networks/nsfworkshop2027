Overview

Computing systems across all scales – from mobile devices to supercomputers – are increasingly transitioning to chiplet-based architectures and advanced 3D packaging techniques to sustain performance and energy efficiency gains in the wake of the slowing of Moore’s Law. Concurrently, applications like Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning are expanding at an exponential pace, placing unprecedented demands on compute, memory, and communication. While individual domains have begun exploring chiplets, hardware vendors have largely implemented these technologies in isolation. Without a coordinated, cross-layer transformation, modern systems will fail to fully exploit the potential of emerging hardware packaging.

This invitation-only, two-day workshop, organized by a team from Cornell University and the University of Wisconsin–Madison, assembles a diverse set of stakeholders from academia, industry, and the national labs to articulate key challenges and create a cross-cutting, near- and long-term actionable plan for the community to pursue to design efficient, scalable, chiplet-rich systems.

The workshop aims to fundamentally rethink data movement as a first-class design principle through cross-layer co-design of networking fabrics, applications, operating systems, runtimes, and compute architectures — spanning scale-in communication within chips and packages, scale-up interconnects connecting hosts and accelerators, scale-out networking across racks and clusters, and scale-across communication linking datacenters and geographically distributed infrastructure. Sessions will cover:

  1. Chiplets, semiconductors, and packaging trends
  2. Architectural challenges to adopting chiplets at scale
  3. Managing chiplets in operating systems and runtimes
  4. Evolving networks for chiplet-based systems
  5. The role of applications in chiplet-rich systems
  6. Chiplets for quantum computing systems
  7. A community roadmap for near- and long-term research directions

The workshop is generously funded by the National Science Foundation, Award #2629392.
